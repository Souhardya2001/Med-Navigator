Title: Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)

Overview
Hypertension is a common medical condition in which blood pressure remains higher than the normal range over time. Blood pressure is expressed using systolic and diastolic measurements. Persistent high blood pressure can place extra stress on the heart and blood vessels.

Common Risk Factors
Risk factors may include family history, increasing age, excess body weight, physical inactivity, high dietary sodium intake, tobacco use, excessive alcohol consumption, and chronic stress. Some medical conditions and medications can also contribute to elevated blood pressure.

Possible Symptoms
Hypertension often does not cause noticeable symptoms, which is why it is sometimes called a silent condition. When blood pressure becomes extremely high, a person may experience severe headache, vision changes, chest discomfort, shortness of breath, weakness, or confusion.

Diagnosis and Monitoring
Blood pressure is commonly measured with an automated or manual blood pressure cuff. A diagnosis generally requires measurements taken on more than one occasion because blood pressure can vary throughout the day. Healthcare professionals may also assess kidney function, cardiovascular risk, and other relevant health factors.

Management
Management may include regular physical activity, maintaining a healthy weight, reducing excessive sodium intake, limiting alcohol, avoiding tobacco, improving sleep, and taking prescribed medication when necessary. Common medication classes include ACE inhibitors, ARBs, calcium-channel blockers, and diuretics.

Important Note
A person should not start, stop, or change blood-pressure medication without discussing it with a qualified healthcare professional. Very high blood pressure accompanied by symptoms such as chest pain, severe shortness of breath, confusion, or neurological changes requires urgent medical evaluation.
