Title: Asthma

Overview
Asthma is a chronic respiratory condition involving inflammation and increased sensitivity of the airways. During an asthma episode, the airways can narrow and produce additional mucus, making it harder for air to move in and out of the lungs.

Common Symptoms
Typical symptoms include wheezing, coughing, shortness of breath, and chest tightness. Symptoms may occur intermittently or become more frequent depending on the person's asthma pattern and exposure to triggers.

Common Triggers
Asthma triggers vary between individuals. Examples include dust mites, pollen, mold, animal allergens, smoke, air pollution, respiratory infections, cold air, exercise, and strong odors. Identifying personal triggers can help with asthma management.

Diagnosis
Diagnosis usually involves a medical history, physical examination, and lung-function testing such as spirometry. Additional testing may sometimes be used to evaluate airway responsiveness or alternative causes of respiratory symptoms.

Treatment
Asthma treatment is commonly based on inhaled medications. Quick-relief medicines may be used for sudden symptoms, while controller medicines are taken regularly to reduce airway inflammation and prevent symptoms. Inhaled corticosteroids are an important controller treatment for many people with persistent asthma.

Asthma Action Plans
People with asthma may benefit from a written asthma action plan that explains their daily medicines, how to recognize worsening symptoms, and what steps to take during an exacerbation. Correct inhaler technique is also important.

Emergency Warning Signs
Severe difficulty breathing, inability to speak normally because of breathlessness, bluish lips or face, confusion, or rapidly worsening symptoms can indicate a medical emergency. Emergency medical care should be sought when severe symptoms do not improve with the person's prescribed rescue treatment.

Important Note
Asthma treatment should be individualized. People should follow the treatment plan provided by their healthcare professional and should not change prescribed inhalers without medical advice.
