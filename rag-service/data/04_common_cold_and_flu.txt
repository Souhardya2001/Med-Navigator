Title: Common Cold and Influenza (Flu)

Overview
The common cold and influenza are viral respiratory illnesses. They can share symptoms, but influenza often begins more suddenly and may cause more pronounced fever, body aches, fatigue, and headache.

Common Cold Symptoms
Cold symptoms may include a runny or blocked nose, sneezing, sore throat, mild cough, and general tiredness. Fever is less common in adults with an uncomplicated cold.

Influenza Symptoms
Flu may cause fever or chills, cough, sore throat, muscle or body aches, headache, and significant fatigue. Some people, particularly children, may also experience vomiting or diarrhea.

Transmission
Respiratory viruses can spread when an infected person coughs, sneezes, talks, or releases respiratory particles. Viruses may also spread when contaminated hands or surfaces are followed by touching the eyes, nose, or mouth.

General Care
Most uncomplicated viral respiratory infections improve with rest, adequate fluid intake, and appropriate symptom management. Staying home when sick and practicing good hand hygiene can reduce transmission to other people.

Prevention
Preventive measures include regular handwashing, improving indoor ventilation, avoiding close contact with people who are ill when possible, and following recommended vaccination guidance. Influenza vaccination can reduce the risk of influenza and its complications.

When to Seek Medical Care
Medical evaluation may be appropriate for severe or worsening symptoms, persistent high fever, dehydration, difficulty breathing, chest pain, confusion, or symptoms that improve and then become significantly worse. People at higher risk of complications may need medical advice earlier.

Important Note
Antibiotics do not treat viral infections such as the common cold or influenza. A healthcare professional can determine whether testing or antiviral treatment is appropriate in a particular situation.
